The Women's Forum for Human Rights and Democracy (WOFHRAD-SL) is based in Makeni, Sierra Leone. We work for the promotion and protection of human rights, justice and democracy, with a focus on the rights of women and girls. We run activities and training for women, go into schools to teach children about women's rights, and monitor local courts to make sure they run as they should. Our ultimate aim is to promote a culture of peace.

## Why WOFHRAD-SL was founded

WOFHRAD-SL was founded in 2006 by a group of young women who were all students at the University of Makeni. They were inspired by the Vice Chancellor at the time, Rev. Fr. Dr Joe Turay, who told students the university was "not grooming job seekers but job creators".

At the same time, Isha Kanu-Moriba was working with a United Nations mission in Sierra Leone and visiting communities where she saw women denied their basic human rights. She had always wanted to improve the lives of women and girls, and she brought together a group of women to set up an organisation to tackle the problems women and girls faced in the north of the country. In doing so, the founders fulfilled their Vice Chancellor's hope: they created jobs for others and brought positive change to their society.

## Our first projects

After registering with the Ministry of Social Welfare, Gender and Children's Affairs, WOFHRAD-SL secured funding from the Justice and Peace Commission to teach secondary school pupils about the report of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, which examined the causes of the war and made recommendations that led to many development programmes. Many people did not know what the report was or that it existed. WOFHRAD-SL visited 10 secondary schools in Makeni to explain it to pupils and teachers, and asked them to share what they learned with their families.

Next came "Facilitating Access to Justice for Survivors of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence", funded by the Justice Sector Development Programme, in Safroko Limba chiefdom, Bombali District. WOFHRAD-SL paid survivors' transport from their rural homes to the police stations and courts in the larger towns, so women who would otherwise have been unable to report crimes against them could seek justice.

During a national election, with funding from ENCISS, WOFHRAD-SL ran training and outreach in all 13 chiefdoms of Bombali District. The project worked with young people on peacebuilding, the causes and effects of violence, and why they should stay out of election violence.

In 2013, WOFHRAD-SL began a one-year project with ActionAid International on women's right to land.
