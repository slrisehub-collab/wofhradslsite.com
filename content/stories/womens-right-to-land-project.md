With ActionAid International, WOFHRAD-SL has run a project to raise awareness of women's right to own land and to build the skills of women farmers in rural areas.

This work matters because very few women in Sierra Leone own land. That leaves them vulnerable: they work the land to feed their families but have no control over it. Women are routinely left out of decisions about how land is used or sold, so they can suddenly lose their income with no safety net.

## Community outreach

We began with community outreach in Sella Limba and Magbaimba Ndowahun chiefdoms, Bombali District. We brought key stakeholders together, making sure women were well represented, for a talk on why women's ownership of land matters and on women's rights and access to land.

## Training for women farmers

In the same area, we then ran two days of training for 20 rural women farmers on basic agronomic practices, seed bank management and post-harvest management. The women said they were excited to use the new techniques and looked forward to better harvests.

## Registering six women farmers' associations

Next, we helped six women farmers' associations in Bombali Shebora, Sella Limba and Magbaimba Ndowahun chiefdoms register with the District Council, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Food Security, and the Ministry of Social Welfare, Gender and Children's Affairs.

We visited each community to learn how they farm and how they run their meetings, then helped the groups write their by-laws and take minutes. Each group also needed a bank account, which meant passport photographs of the account holders; luckily, one of our volunteers had a camera. WOFHRAD-SL placed Le 250,000 (old Leones) in each account to help the groups get started. We finished with a ceremony to hand certificates to each group's representatives. The women were delighted that their organisations were now properly registered.

## A public lecture in Makeni

The project ended with a public lecture on women's right to land on 6 July 2013 at the Sierra Leone Teachers Union hall in Makeni. Invitations went out late, and we worried about turnout, but 121 people came. The main lecture was given by Mr Marah, a human rights officer with UNIPSIL in Makeni. Representatives of other groups also shared their colleagues' views, including the Makeni Police, the Sierra Leone Armed Forces, teachers, chiefdom councillors, secondary and tertiary students, and the Council of Churches and Imams.

Feedback was positive. We hope everyone who attended will talk to friends and colleagues about women's right to land, and that it marks the beginning of a change in attitudes.
