Last week we welcomed four new VSO ICS volunteers to the team: Charlotte Langridge and Dhakshi Suriar from the UK, and Kadiatu Kargbo and Emmanuel K. Kamanda from Sierra Leone. They will work on our existing projects and bring their own ideas, carrying on the work of our previous volunteers, Salifu Kanu (Sierra Leone) and Sarah Ludbrook (UK), in raising awareness of women's and children's rights in local schools and communities.

## At the Magistrate's Court

In their first week, WOFHRAD-SL co-founder Emilia Kamara took the team to meet Alhaji Stevens at the Makeni Magistrate's Court, where they sat in on some cases. It was a particularly interesting experience for the UK volunteers: in Sierra Leone, women may not wear trousers in the Magistrate's Court, and it is customary for them to cover their hair. One case involved two men accused of embezzling goods worth Le 1 billion (old Leones) from a large construction company in Makeni. The team hope to monitor court cases in future, particularly those involving women and gender-based violence.

## A public meeting on investment and development

The volunteers also attended a validation meeting at Makeni City Hall, where the final Constraints Analysis Report was presented by the Millennium Challenge Coordinating Unit (MCCU), which works with the US Government's Millennium Challenge Corporation. After prayers and welcomes from the Chief Administrator, the Paramount Chief and Professor Strasser-King, the MCCU Coordinator gave an overview of the report.

The report looks at the main barriers to the foreign investment that drives economic growth, and the conditions Sierra Leone must improve on to keep receiving support. These include civil liberties, the rule of law, freedom of information, protection of the environment and access to credit for small businesses. In particular, it highlighted infrastructure, water and sanitation, and above all corruption.

On electricity, new distribution and generation authorities had been set up to regulate and improve supply, with the aim of extending it from three or four areas of the country to nine to eleven. A project to improve access to safe water and sanitation in the Western Area was estimated at around US$150 million, which the meeting described as the largest of its kind in Sierra Leone.

Corruption was the issue of most concern. In 2013, Sierra Leone did not meet the control-of-corruption standard, which is assessed using external sources such as Transparency International and Freedom House. Steps mentioned at the meeting included a freedom of information law and the "Pay No Bribe" campaign against corruption in government offices.

Next week the volunteers will attend a Training of Trainers session led by ActionAid, WOFHRAD-SL's main funder at the time, and an accountability session led by ENCISS. Both will prepare them for the sessions they plan to run in the coming weeks.
